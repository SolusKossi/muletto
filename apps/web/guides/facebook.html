How to export your data from Facebook

easy Wait: minutes to a few days depending on size

Facebook is required to give you a copy of the data it holds about you, including photos, messages and posts and social activity. The request itself is free, and arrives as .zip containing JSON or HTML files and media. Typical wait: minutes to a few days depending on size.

Meta Accounts Center - Download your information

JSON, not HTML, and it is not obvious why

Meta offers both. HTML looks friendlier - it opens in a browser and resembles Facebook - but it is a rendering of your data rather than the data, and nothing can reliably read it back. JSON is the machine-readable one, and it is what Muletto reads. Pick HTML and you have a scrapbook you can only look at; pick JSON and you have your data, which Muletto turns into something you can look at anyway.

Step by step

  1. Open Download your information

    Go to Accounts Center, then Your information and permissions, then Download your information. Sign in if asked.

  2. Choose the Facebook profile

    If your accounts are linked, select the Facebook profile whose data you want.

  3. Select information and range

    Set the date range to All time unless you have a reason not to. Meta defaults to a recent window on some screens, and a range quietly left at twelve months produces an export that looks complete and is missing most of your life. Tick everything you care about; posts, photos and messages are separate selections.

  4. Pick format and quality

    Choose JSON for the format. For media quality, High keeps your photographs at full size and roughly doubles the archive - often past 12 GB for a long-standing account - while Medium is far smaller and permanently smaller in quality. If this export is the copy you intend to keep, choose High: it is the only chance to get the originals out.

  5. Create the file and wait

    Submit the request. Meta prepares the download and notifies you when it is ready.

  6. Download the file

    The link is emailed and expires in a few days, so download it promptly. Save every part if it arrives split, keep them in one folder, and do not unzip - Muletto reads the archives directly.

Worth knowing

Facebook and Instagram both live in Meta Accounts Center, so you can request them from the same place.
Choose JSON if you plan to search or organize the data - it is far cleaner to work with than the HTML version.
Meta produces this export for Facebook and Instagram separately, even when the accounts are linked. Request each one.
HTML exports cannot be read back by Muletto or by anything else. If you already have one, request JSON as well - it costs another wait and nothing else.
The wait is usually minutes to hours rather than days, but Meta gives no progress and the email is the only signal.

Common questions

Why does my Facebook export show strange characters instead of letters?

Facebook writes JSON by escaping the individual bytes of each character rather than the character itself, so anything outside plain English arrives broken. Norwegian, Swedish, German and every emoji are affected. The text is not damaged, only mis-encoded, and it can be repaired exactly. Muletto does this automatically.

Should I choose JSON or HTML for my Facebook download?

JSON if you intend to open it with anything other than a browser, because it keeps the structure. HTML is easier to skim by hand and much harder to read programmatically. Muletto reads the JSON.

How long does a Facebook data download take?

Anything from minutes to several days depending on how much there is. The download link stays live only a few days once it arrives, so fetch it promptly.

Why is my Facebook export split into multiple files?

Meta splits large downloads at around four gigabytes. The parts are slices of one folder tree rather than copies, so extract all of them into the same place before opening, or whole sections will look as though they are missing.

What Muletto does with this export

Once your download arrives, open it in Muletto. The file is read in your browser, on your own machine - nothing is uploaded. You get a breakdown of what is inside, duplicate detection, and a browsable view of your photos, messages, timeline and records.
