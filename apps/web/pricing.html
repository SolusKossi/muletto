Free, apart from the bit that costs money

Everything Muletto does on your own machine is free. There is no tier, no licence key and no account. The only thing charged for is AI, because that is the only thing with a cost each time you use it.

The guides

Step-by-step instructions for getting your data out of every major service, kept current and walked through by hand.

Opening and reading it

Any export, several at once, merged into one library. A timeline, your pictures, your conversations across services, a map.

Cleaning it up

Exact copies and near-copies found across every service, and you decide what a tidied library keeps.

Repairing it

The real capture date and place written back into each photo, so whatever you move it into afterwards sorts it correctly.

Getting it out

Dated folders straight onto a drive or a NAS, or one archive. Arranged how you want it, with an index of everything written.

Keeping your place

Your library waits for you between visits, and work already done is never done twice - even against next year's export.

What AI costs

Describing a photo, searching by what is in a picture rather than its filename, or summarising years of conversation - these run a model, and running a model costs money every single time. That cost is real, so it is passed on rather than hidden in a subscription.

Credits, at a published rate

One credit describes one photo, at one published price. You buy the number of photos you actually have rather than picking a tier, the panel shows the total before you spend anything, and whatever you do not use stays as credit. Nothing recurring, nothing to cancel, nothing expires.

Or bring your own model

Point Muletto at a model running on your own machine and the same features cost nothing at all. It needs a reasonably recent computer with memory to spare, and it will be slower than the hosted option.

Coming later: a desktop app

Windows and macOS, and it will be paid. Not a wall around the web version - the web version stays exactly as it is. It is for libraries the browser struggles with: no storage limits, no file size ceiling, direct access to your disks and network drives, work that carries on in the background, and no internet at all if you would rather.
